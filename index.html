About

A little about my work

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Health Metrics Science at the University of Washington, with adjunct appointments in Global Health and Environmental & Occupational Health Sciences. At the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME), I lead research on environmental and occupational risk factors within the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, spanning air pollution, temperature, and 24 other risk factors, and have spearheaded the first-ever global, cause-specific estimates of the burden of non-optimal temperature.

My research also extends to climate change projections and a newer program of work estimating the burden of viral diseases such as influenza and RSV, adapting the GBD's risk-assessment framework to population-level viral surveillance data. This work has been published in journals including The Lancet, Nature Aging, and JACC, and featured in outlets such as CNN and Scientific American.

Alongside research, I teach and mentor graduate students in Global Health and Health Metrics Science, and serve as an international expert with the WHO–WMO Global Heat Health Information Network and as a contributing author to the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report. Diversity, equity, and inclusion are central to how I teach, mentor, and design my research.
