Research Area

Real-World Evidence

Turning large, real-world datasets into risk-attributable burden estimates that decision-makers can act on.

← Back to Research

Real world data — clinical records, disease surveillance systems, electronic health records, and other routinely collected sources — capture how exposures and disease actually play out in populations, at a scale and level of detail that published literature alone often can't offer. Much of my work turns these large, real world datasets into evidence that decision makers can act on. Within the GBD study, I translate population level exposure and outcome data into risk attributable burden estimates for 204 countries and territories. For our research on suboptimal temperature, we derived exposure response functions from a dataset of 65 million individual deaths — rather than relying on a limited set of published studies — to produce globally coherent, cause specific burden estimates.
