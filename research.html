Research

Turning data into public health evidence

My research uncovers the environmental and occupational determinants that shape human health in a changing world, and translates that evidence into policy-relevant guidance. At the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation, I lead work on risk assessment within the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, with a growing focus on climate change and the burden of infectious disease.

Focus Areas

What I research

Risk Assessment

I lead methods development for 24 of the GBD study's 88 risk factors, from air pollution and temperature to occupational carcinogens and ergonomic factors — including a complete overhaul of the household air pollution model published in The Lancet.

Read more →

Environmental Health

A large share of my research quantifies how the air we breathe affects our health — from childhood asthma linked to NO₂ exposure to dementia risk from long-term PM2.5 exposure, published in Nature Aging.

Read more →

Occupational Health

I oversee GBD estimates for occupational carcinogens, asthmagens, noise, injuries, and ergonomic factors, and supervise doctoral research on occupational cancer and mortality burden.

Read more →

Climate and Health

I led the first cause-specific, globally coherent estimates of the burden of non-optimal temperature, published in The Lancet — 1.87 million deaths attributable in 2023 — and am now projecting how climate change will reshape that burden.

Read more →

Infectious Diseases

A newer strand of my research adapts the GBD's risk-assessment framework to estimate the burden of viral diseases — influenza, RSV, and dengue — whose true impact is often underestimated in existing surveillance.

Read more →

Real-World Evidence

Much of my work turns large, real-world datasets — including a 65-million-death dataset for our temperature research — into risk-attributable burden estimates for 204 countries that decision-makers can act on.

Read more →

Extreme Temperature and Aging

As PI of a five-year, $3.7 million NIA/NIH R01 grant, I'm mapping present and future temperature-attributable health risk across U.S. counties, by age, race, and ethnicity — and evaluating which interventions can reduce it.
