Teaching & Mentorship

Training the next generation of health scientists

Teaching students to critically evaluate evidence and translate it into public health impact is one of the parts of my job I value most. My approach has been shaped by moving between academic systems, teaching through the disruptions of COVID-19, and a continued focus on building inclusive, supportive classrooms for students from all backgrounds.

Philosophy

How I approach teaching

As someone who identifies first as a scientist, I have worked consciously to translate a research-driven mindset into teaching strategies that resonate with diverse learners, and I find real fulfillment in watching students grow in confidence and independence over a course or across their academic trajectory. Diversity, equity, and inclusion are a cornerstone of my teaching and mentoring. As a female scientist from a working-class background, a non-native English speaker, and a member of a bi-racial, multi-ethnic family, I draw on my own experience of feeling different from the perceived "norm" in academic spaces to build classrooms where students feel seen, valued, and empowered — regardless of background.

Courses

What I teach

Since joining the University of Washington, I have taught four courses spanning Health Metrics Science, Global Health, and Population Health Informatics, at both the graduate and undergraduate level.

4.8–5.0 / 5

HMS 530 — Global Burden of Disease: Methods and Results

Graduate · Spring 2024–2026

Shares in-depth knowledge of GBD methods and risk assessment, covering comprehensive methods and real-world applications that build students' understanding of global health metrics.

4.6 / 5

GH 592B — Health Metrics Science Seminar

Graduate journal club · Winter, Spring & Autumn 2019, Winter 2020

Familiarizes graduate students with a diverse range of scientific literature, building skills to critically evaluate burden estimates, demographic and economic analyses, and methodological papers — including through a diversity, equity, and inclusion lens.

Up to 4.9 / 5

GH 511 — Problems in Global Health

Graduate, online MPH curriculum · Summer 2022–2024, Winter 2023–2025

Introduces MPH students to Global Health, exploring how political, socioeconomic, cultural, and demographic conditions in low- and middle-income countries shape health and health services.

INFO 478 — Population Health Informatics

Undergraduate, Information School · Spring 2021, Winter 2022

Introduces undergraduates to strategies and techniques for evaluating population health, using real data sources to build epidemiological, analytical, and data visualization skills. Taught remotely and in hybrid format during the COVID-19 pandemic.

I have also led internal trainings at IHME, including the GBD Learning Week (November 2025) and the GBD Foundations Workshop (February 2026).
