Research Area

Occupational Health

Estimating disease burden from workplace exposures — carcinogens, asthmagens, noise, injuries, and ergonomic factors.

← Back to Research

Within the GBD risk factor portfolio, I oversee estimates for occupational carcinogens, asthmagens, noise, injuries, and ergonomic factors — risks that accumulate quietly over a working lifetime and are often under-recognized relative to their eventual toll. I supervise doctoral research examining the burden of lung cancer and mortality attributable to occupational risk factors in Brazil and other countries, and mentor burden-of-proof assessments of the carcinogenic effects of workplace exposures, strengthening the evidence base linking specific occupational hazards to long-term disease risk.

Asbestos exposure has been a particular focus of this work. Despite being banned or restricted in many countries, asbestos remains in use across parts of the Americas, and its cancer-causing effects — mesothelioma, and lung, laryngeal, and ovarian cancers — can take decades to manifest, making the true scale of its burden easy to underestimate. I contributed to Burden of Cancer Attributable to Occupational Asbestos Exposure in the Americas, 1990–2023: An Analysis Using the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023, published in The Lancet Regional Health – Americas. The study found stark regional and sex disparities: High-income North America carried the highest burden in 2023, at 5.1 deaths and 84.9 DALYs per 100,000, while Southern Latin America had the second-highest rates and the sharpest rise in burden among women, increasing by 2.3% annually in both mortality and DALYs.

This kind of evidence is what allows occupational exposures to be treated as a modifiable, policy-relevant risk rather than an unavoidable cost of work — informing where regulation, workplace protections, and continued phase-out of hazardous materials like asbestos can have the greatest effect.
